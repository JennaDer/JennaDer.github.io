Where supply chain meets data
The weakest decisions I have seen were driven by metrics that looked precise and measured the wrong thing.

That is why I am completing a Master of Science in Supply Chain Management with a concentration in Data Analytics at Boston University (December 2026). I never set out to be an engineer. I wanted to be fluent enough to stand between the people who build data tools and the people who depend on them: to follow how information moves through SAP and out to suppliers, and to design KPIs that reflect how an operation really runs.

What shapes my work

A market that does not stand still.

Complexity

Semiconductors are among the most complex products in the world to make and to move. One component can cross several countries before it reaches a production line, and capacity cannot be added quickly.

Artificial intelligence

Demand for AI hardware has redrawn the market, shifting where capacity goes and changing availability and pricing for everyone else.

Geopolitics

Trade policy, export controls, and tariffs now influence sourcing decisions as much as price and lead time do.
